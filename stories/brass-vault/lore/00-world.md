**The City of Vellmoor**

Vellmoor is a harbor city of brass, soot and steam, built in terraces up the side of a cold grey bay. Coal smoke hangs over the rooftops so thickly that the gas lamps on its streets burn day and night. Above the smog, airships drift between iron mooring masts, carrying tea, coal and secrets to the far colonies. The year is 1887 by the city's own calendar. There is no magic in Vellmoor, only engineering: clockwork automatons, pneumatic post tubes, steam boilers, difference engines that punch holes into brass cards, and the occasional inventor who claims otherwise.

The city is ruled in practice by money. The Ashcombe Mercantile Bank holds the debts of half the shipyards and most of the airship captains. Its director, Silas Ashcombe, is the grandson of the founder and the most feared man on the bay.

**The Clockheart**

Deep under the bank lies the Orrery Vault, a chamber sealed by a lock built in the shape of a great brass orrery. Inside the vault rests the Clockheart: a fist-sized engine of crystal and gold gears that never stops turning and never needs winding. Its inventor, the late Professor Amara Quell, died in debt to the bank, and the bank seized her life's work. Tomorrow at noon the Clockheart will be sold at a private auction to the Admiralty, who mean to build war-dreadnoughts that never land.

Tonight the bank hosts its annual Founders' Gala. The doors are open to the rich, the guards are distracted by champagne, and the boilers run hot to warm the ballroom. It is the one night the Clockheart can be stolen.

**The Crew**

The heist is planned by Mags Holloway, a former airship captain who lost her ship to the bank. She has recruited the player: a freelance specialist new to Vellmoor, hired for skills Mags does not have. The crew meets in the back room of The Rusty Gear Tavern. The plan is simple to say and hard to do: get into the bank, open the Orrery Vault, take the Clockheart, and reach Mooring Mast Nine before dawn, where an airship waits to carry it out of the city.

**How the world works**

- People in Vellmoor are human. There are no elves, dwarves or other peoples; travellers from the colonies are human too. Anyone claiming supernatural powers is a fraud or a stage performer.
- Technology is Victorian steam age: revolvers and pepperbox pistols exist but are loud and rare; most guards carry truncheons and whistles. Electricity is a laboratory curiosity. There are no radios, only the pneumatic post and messenger boys.
- Clockwork automatons follow punched brass cards. An automaton obeys whoever presents the right card, and it can be confused by a strong surge of steam pressure in the building's pipes.
- The city watch is underpaid. Some constables can be bribed; the honest ones are dangerous.
- Money is counted in crowns and pennies. A good meal costs two pennies; an airship passage costs fifty crowns.
- Violence draws the watch quickly. The best heists in Vellmoor are the ones nobody notices until morning.
