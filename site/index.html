How it works

A book that plays with you

Every story is built from its lore into a real world — places, things and people linked together. The game master is an AI that can change that world only through fair moves, so it cannot cheat for you, or against you.

  1. 01

    Bring a hero

    Describe anyone — an elven archivist, a retired soldier, a broke actor. The story fits them into its world and offers three versions to choose from.

  2. 02

    Say anything

    Type what you do, as you would tell a friend. Talk, sneak, bargain, lie. The master narrates; the people you meet answer in their own voice.

  3. 03

    The world remembers

    Take the lantern and it is in your pocket tomorrow. Anger a guard and he will not forget. Your save is the world itself, not a summary.

Example turns

What a turn looks like

Moments like these from The Brass Vault, a steampunk heist in the city of Vellmoor, 1887.

Lirael. An elf, 140 years old — a wandering archivist who reads the stars.
There are no elves in Vellmoor — but there are people the city never quite explains. Here are three ways you could belong:
The Star-ReaderHuman, 28. An archivist from a far island who charts the sky for the Observatory.
The Old ClerkHuman, 61. Keeps the Admiralty’s star charts — and its secrets.
The FoundlingPale, ageless, nobody’s. Rumours call you fae.

The story keeps your idea and fits it to its world. Three changes allowed, then you pick.

I ask Pip what he knows about the bank’s pneumatic tubes.
Pip“Tubes? Every message in that bank goes through ’em. Main line runs under Lamplight Row and comes up in the sorting room. Wide enough for a cat. Or a very small thief.” He grins and looks at you sideways.
Pip pockets the coin you didn’t offer yet, as if it were already his.

Each character has their own persona, memory and knowledge. Pip knows the sewers; Mags knows people.

I pull out my smartphone and call the police.
Your hand closes on the brass lantern — the only thing you carry. In 1887 the nearest constable is reached by shouting, and Mags gives you a look that says shouting would be the last mistake you make tonight.

Things that do not exist in the world do not appear. A gentle no, in the voice of the story.

I quickly snatch the calling card from Pip’s fingers.
Luck: 4 of 20 — not tonightPip’s hand is quicker than it looks. The card vanishes up his sleeve, and your fingers close on air.
Pip“Manners, guv. That’ll cost you double now.”

Bold moves roll for luck. The master does not give in to the player — people have their own will.
